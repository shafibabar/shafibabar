How I Research

Find out what's true before building

Before a team commits to a build, I work out what is true: from customers, from the system itself, and from small experiments. Every example here comes from my resume or from the repositories behind my recent builds.

Evidence standards

Rules I write into the products and the build instructions, so the standard holds when I'm not in the room.

Observability Copilot charter

Label every claim

Each statement is a Fact, a Hypothesis, a Recommendation, or an Unknown, so readers always know what was observed and what was inferred. "Never present speculation as certainty."

Observability Copilot charter

Look for what would disprove it

Every hypothesis must carry the evidence against it and the information still missing. Those are required fields, not optional extras.

podcast-skills rules

No fabricated precision

Never invent benchmarks, prices, dates, counts, or sources. If a value can't be verified, omit it or say it couldn't be verified.

podcast-skills rules

Date what can change

Claims that depend on a version carry that version and the date they were checked, and are flagged for review rather than silently updated.

This site follows the same rule: where I haven't supplied the words, you'll see a marked content slot instead of invented copy.

Methods

Six ways I find out what's true, each with a real example.

Customers and stakeholders

At Data Dynamics I shaped the InsightAnalytiX roadmap directly around each client's requirements, growing it to three enterprise customers and about $4.5M in annual contract value. With CXO stakeholders, I walked through strategic options and trade-offs to align the roadmap with business objectives.

Content slot. How you run discovery: cadence, who you talk to, how you synthesize, and how findings reach the backlog.

Research the system before modeling it

For the Enterprise Conduct explainer, I built a 2,273-line knowledge base from the platform's own channel tables, a connectivity audit, and service documentation. Every connection in the model was traced from source rather than guessed, and anything that couldn't be measured is labeled as assumed.

Spikes before commitments

podcast-skills started with five spikes on day one (headless agent runs, worktrees versus branches, a UI panel library, YAML round trips, and Windows support) before any feature work. A real run was then measured at about 69 seconds and US$0.25, and the notes say plainly where something was not yet verified.

Open questions, closed into decisions

In Observability Copilot, anything unresolved goes into an open-questions log that states what is unverified and what would settle it. When it's answered, it moves into a dated decision record. At last count: 6 open, 4 resolved.

Know the audience

The Observability Copilot charter defines five personas and what each needs first. A site reliability engineer, for example, leads with golden signals, SLOs, error-budget burn, and root-cause hypotheses. The same facts are then explained through each lens.

Public-source analysis

Case studies use public information only, and every claim traces to a listed source. They are labeled as independent analysis. See the case studies.

From question to decision

The loop those methods share.

  1. Log the questionWhat's unknown, and what would settle it
  2. Pick the cheapest testA source trace, a spike, or a customer conversation
  3. Label the evidenceFact, hypothesis, or unknown
  4. Record the decisionDated, and amended rather than deleted
  5. RevisitWhen the evidence changes
