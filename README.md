- Helping shape product strategy and target-state architecture: looking past the next release to think about where the platform needs to be in the next few years, and working backward from there
- Acting as a thought partner to engineering and leadership: contributing a point of view on technology direction, build-vs-buy trade-offs, and platform investment, not just feature requests

- Building alignment across stakeholders with different priorities: engineering, compliance/legal, and business — around a shared long-term direction
- Staying close enough to execution to keep strategy grounded in reality, without losing sight of the bigger picture

### NFRs I care about

Non-functional requirements rarely get the same attention as features, but in regulated industries they often determine whether a product actually holds up. A few I consistently prioritize:

- **Security & data privacy**: protecting sensitive financial and health data (PII/PHI) by design, not as an afterthought
- **Auditability & traceability**: being able to reconstruct who did what, when, and why is often as important as the feature itself
- **Reliability & availability**: downtime in financial or healthcare systems has real consequences, so resilience planning starts early
- **Data integrity & accuracy**: for compliance decisions and clinical workflows alike, the data has to be trustworthy, not just available
- **Interoperability**: standards-based integration (APIs, HL7/FHIR) shapes how well a platform fits into a customer's existing ecosystem
- **Observability**: visibility into production behavior helps catch issues before they become compliance or safety incidents
- **Scalability**: platforms need to hold up as usage and data volume grow, not just perform well at launch
